Guide to getting a secondary Linux machine for the dissertation — SHU loan scheme and secondhand market options

The SHU laptop loan scheme — what it actually is
SHU's loan scheme is NOT what you're thinking. It's a short-term library loan — 1 day or 1 week maximum, from lockers at Adsetts and Collegiate libraries, wiped after every return. You cannot install software on them, you cannot keep them for the dissertation period, and they are standard Windows library machines — not research-grade hardware. This will not work as your secondary Linux machine.
SHU Library Loan — avoid for this
Library laptop locker
1-day or 1-week loan only. Wiped regularly — no persistent software. Available 24/7 at Adsetts & Collegiate. Useful for Word/browser work on campus. Useless for installing Docker, Python, GMAT, or cFS.
SHU IT — worth asking
Hallam Help / IT Services
Email hallamhelp@shu.ac.uk and ask specifically if extended hardware loans exist for postgraduate dissertation students. Some UK universities do have longer-term loans for PGT students — SHU may have a scheme not publicly advertised. Worth a 5-minute email.
SHU Labs — use freely
Campus computer labs
Cantor building labs have workstations with more power than library laptops. Check if any labs have Linux dual-boot or if you can request Linux access via IT. Your SDL sessions are in Cantor — ask the IT desk directly.
Real answer for your secondary machine: secondhand market. For £120–300 you can get a machine that runs Linux perfectly, handles all 4 dissertation phases, and is yours to keep and configure. This is a far better outcome than a borrowed machine you'd return in September anyway.
Minimum specs you actually need for this dissertation on Linux
CPU Intel Core i5 6th gen or newer (or AMD Ryzen 5 2000 series+). You need AVX2 instruction support for PyTorch — everything 6th gen Intel and Ryzen onwards has it. Don't go older.
RAM 16 GB minimum. Non-negotiable — running Docker (cFS), GMAT, and a Jupyter notebook simultaneously will hit 12 GB easily. 32 GB is ideal but not essential.
Storage 256 GB SSD minimum. An SSD is mandatory — GMAT and Docker on a spinning HDD is genuinely painful. 512 GB preferred. NVMe is faster but SATA SSD is fine.
GPU (for CNN) NVIDIA GPU strongly preferred if you want local GPU training. GTX 1060 6GB or better for CUDA support. AMD GPUs work but PyTorch CUDA support is NVIDIA-only — AMD uses ROCm which is finicky on Linux. If no GPU, use Google Colab — no problem.
Display 1080p minimum. If it's a laptop, 14" or 15.6" is fine. If it's a desktop, you'll need a monitor — factor that into your budget.
Linux compatibility ThinkPads, Dell Latitudes, and HP EliteBooks are the gold standard — all have excellent Linux driver support, documented community knowledge, and easily replaceable parts. Avoid anything with hybrid graphics (NVIDIA Optimus on budget machines) unless you're confident with drivers.

Where to buy secondhand in the UK
eBay UK
Biggest selection. Filter: "Used", "Seller in United Kingdom". Buy from sellers with 99%+ feedback and 200+ reviews. Check "sold" listings to understand real market prices before bidding.
Bargain Hardware
bargainhardware.co.uk — specialist in ex-corporate refurb. Graded (A/B/C). Warranty included. Slightly more than eBay but more reliable. Great for ThinkPads and Latitudes.
Facebook Marketplace
Sheffield local — meet in person, inspect before buying. Often private sellers clearing out old work laptops. Can negotiate. Meet in a public place.
Gumtree
Similar to Marketplace. Filter to Sheffield. Good for quick local deals. Always test before you hand over cash.
Back Market UK
backmarket.co.uk — professionally refurbished, graded, with warranty. Prices slightly higher than eBay but condition is more reliable. Good if you want peace of mind.
SHU Notice Board
Check MyHallam student noticeboard / SHU student Facebook groups — students often sell laptops at end of year. Sheffield student community is active on this.
Which Linux distro to put on it
Ubuntu 24.04 LTS
Best recommendation for you. Every dissertation tool (GMAT, Docker, PyTorch, astropy) has Ubuntu install docs. Huge community. Just works on ThinkPads and Latitudes.
Fedora 40
More cutting-edge, excellent hardware support. Good alternative if Ubuntu feels old. DNF package manager is fast. Strong for developer workloads.
Pop!_OS 22.04
Ubuntu base with better NVIDIA GPU support out-of-the-box. Best choice if the machine has an NVIDIA GPU — auto-installs correct drivers.
Debian 12
Ultra-stable, minimal, long support cycle. Slightly more manual setup. Good if you want a lean machine and are comfortable with Linux already.
What to check before buying any secondhand machine
Battery healthAsk for "battery wear percentage" — under 20% wear is good. Over 50% wear means it won't last long off-mains. Replaceable on ThinkPads for ~£20.
Screen conditionCheck for dead pixels, yellowing, or backlight bleed in photos. Ask seller to take a photo of a plain white screen.
Keyboard / trackpadTest every key. Ex-corporate machines are usually fine but check for sticky or missing keys in photos.
RAM slotsConfirm how many RAM slots and current capacity. T480 has 2 slots — means you can upgrade to 32 GB for ~£25 later.
BIOS unlock / corporate lockSome ex-corporate ThinkPads have BIOS passwords. Ask seller to confirm the BIOS is unlocked and there's no corporate MDM enrollment.
Thermal pasteOn older ThinkPads (5+ years), thermal paste dries out and the machine throttles under load. A repaste (£5 of paste, 20 minutes) fixes this — worth doing before heavy work.
